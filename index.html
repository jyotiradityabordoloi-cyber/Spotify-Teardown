PM case study · Spotify · by Jyotiraditya Bordoloi

Does Spotify listen to you?

Spotify is world-class at replay. When you try to discover something new, it plays your past back to you.

Problem

Radio, shuffle and autoplay recycle songs you already know. Skipping doesn’t change what plays next.

Insight

The dashboard logs a healthy 30-minute session. The listener found zero new songs.

MVP

Playlist Coach: steer the stream you already started, and see why each song plays.

Proof

4-week A/B test. North Star: new-track saves per user per week, +10%.

02 · Who it’s for, and the gap

Built for the Explorer. Nobody serves him today.

Primary user

Arjun, the Explorer

24 · Bengaluru · Premium for 5 years · starts Song Radio to find new artists

  • Needs new artists that still fit the song he started from
  • Hates that every radio drifts back to his Liked Songs
“I didn’t start a radio to hear my own playlist back.”

Also served: the Curator (keep my vibe, add a few) and the Lean-back (must work with zero taps, so smart defaults). Proto-personas from secondary research.

Everyone generates playlists. Nobody steers.

  • SpotifyPrompted Playlist, AI DJ. Radio, shuffle and autoplay stay a black box.
  • YouTube MusicQueue chips steer, but never say why a track was picked.
  • Apple MusicPrompt-made playlists. Nothing helps once music is playing.
  • DeezerFlow Tuner toggles genres, only inside Flow.
THE WHITE SPACETrack-level steering, with a reason, inside the stream you already started.

Trade-offs I made

Why not fix Home first?

Moving discovery up the Home screen raises reach, not quality. If radio still recycles songs, more people just find the loop faster. Fix the radio first, then promote it on Home in v2.

Why keep live coaching at P0?

It scores 4th and is the riskiest bet, at 50% confidence. It is also the one thing no competitor has, so it stays in, behind a canary.

Why cut chat?

Typing to your radio is the flashiest idea and the weakest case: 5 person-months, 40% confidence, and lean-back users won’t type.

Track and artist names in the mockups are invented.

04 · How we’d know it worked

One number to chase, and a check that skips can’t fake

North Star New-track saves per user, per week +10%

It needs both halves: new songs reach you, and you keep them.

Early skips −15%radio, under 30s
Repeat ratio −20%recycled songs
Freshness +15%new, heard 30s+
Save rate +5%new tracks kept
!

Skip rate alone can lie.

A very low skip rate can mean radio only plays familiar songs. Early skips must fall while freshness rises.

The experiment
  • HYPOTHESIS

    If Premium listeners can steer radio, new-track saves per user rise 10%+, because more of what plays is new and a good fit.

  • WHO

    Premium users who start radio, Smart Shuffle or autoplay 2+ times a week. India, US, UK.

  • DESIGN

    50/50 by user, 4 weeks, week 1 read apart for novelty.

  • SAMPLE

    ~6.5K per arm for the North Star; ~200K per arm for the churn guardrail, so we size for churn.

  • GUARDRAILS

    Listening time (no drop past −1%), Premium churn, nudge dismissals, crashes.

  • ROLLOUT

    Dogfood 2 wks → 1% canary → A/B → launch with a 5% holdout for 3 months.

Business targets are deliberately small: +0.5–1% DAU and −5% relative churn are big wins at Spotify’s scale.

05 · Risks and what I’d do first

What could go wrong, and what I’d learn in 30 days

Novelty spikeCoach looks great in week 1, then fades.
→
Read week 1 apart; decide on weeks 2–4.
Lean-back users ignore itChips add effort for people who want none.
→
Smart defaults; Coach only speaks after a skip pattern.
“New” hurts listening timeUnfamiliar songs can end sessions early.
→
Listening time is a guardrail; the dial lets users pull back.
Reasons feel creepy or wrongA bad “why” erodes trust faster than no why.
→
Why this song stays P1; reason quality checked in canary.

Honest limits: one listener’s screens, 51 negative-skewed reviews and an illustrative funnel. The direction is evidence-based; the numbers are assumptions until measured.
